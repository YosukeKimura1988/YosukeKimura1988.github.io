Research

Publications and working papers by Yosuke Kimura

I work at the intersection of corporate finance, information economics, and firm dynamics. My research asks how firms and investors produce, disclose, and act on information under uncertainty — and how those decisions aggregate into market outcomes. Recurring threads include the value of information and data, managerial behavior and cognition, institutional investor engagement, and innovation and corporate networks, studied with both theoretical models and large-scale text and patent data.

Work in Progress

  1. Self-Fulfilling Public Market Access: When Large Equity Markets Are Functionally Thin

    Why can a large stock market fail to finance new growth? This paper distinguishes market size, a stock of claims on existing assets, from public market access, a flow capability that can be crowded out by the very stock of mature listed firms, so that a market becomes large yet functionally thin.

  2. The Market for Credibility: Turbulence, Scrutiny, and the Concentration of Trust

    Why does trust in information concentrate in fewer sources precisely when reliability is hardest to verify? This paper models credibility as a disciplined equilibrium, sustained by franchise value against the temptation to suppress bad news, and shows that turbulence acts as a trust multiplier whose strength, shaped by how strongly audiences reward scrutiny, determines whether trust concentrates smoothly or tips abruptly.

  3. The Geometry of Information Value: Alignment and Endogenous Dimensionality

    Why do high-dimensional decision problems so often give rise to low-dimensional information systems? This paper distinguishes the dimensionality of uncertainty itself from the dimensionality of payoff-relevant uncertainty, showing that optimal information architectures align with the directions that matter most for decision quality and become low-dimensional precisely when payoff relevance is concentrated.
